## Findings

1. The list adds valuable instrument families: bansuri, sitar, santoor,
   shakuhachi, guqin, koto, shamisen, Carnatic violin, tabla, tanpura, and
   Tibetan bowls.
2. Several items are not discoverable under the supplied title/artist pair.
   They must be replaced or corrected before becoming references.
3. “Raga Nilambari induces sleep”, “low guqin lowers cortisol”, “drone
   resonates with the nervous system”, and “tabla forces concentration” are
   not adopted as product claims or generator rules.
4. Tabla, electronic bass, glitch, shamisen attacks, and Lo-fi drums are valid
   only for an explicitly active Focus profile. They are not universal
   meditation or sleep materials.
5. Real cultural instruments need real, rights-cleared sources. A piano or
   generic synth cannot be relabelled as guqin, shakuhachi, sitar, or bansuri.

## Looping implication

These references should inform atomic material and composition rules, not be
looped as whole tracks. The engine needs phrase-boundary loops, instrument and
mode metadata, low-frequency and transient limits, and crossfades matched to
decay: roughly 100-500 ms for short notes, 1-4 seconds for pads, and 2-8
seconds for environmental beds. A stable loop must have no final cadence or
hidden crescendo, and must pass 20-cycle plus 30-minute listening QA.
